Shhhcribble — Studio wireframes

Living set — updated in place as iterations continue. Direction: two halves — the stream of what you said (Dictations) and the shelf of what you keep (Notes, which holds written notes and long-form documents together). Decision record: studio-wireframes.md beside this file.

Last major revision 2026-08-28 (IA rework locked with Hendri; supersedes the five-item shell). Rail is three items on every screen; the titlebar always reads Shhhcribble.

1 · Dictations

Single chronological stream of dictations only — notes and documents both live on the Notes shelf. Rows are borderless lines at full-strength text, always fully expanded — no truncation. Every item's content starts on one left edge; the time sits at the trailing edge, permanently. Copy leads the meta line and is always visible; delete sits far right and appears on hover only (third row shown hovered). Chevrons step a day, and forward is disabled here because the stream is on today — you can't dictate into the future. The day label opens the month popover (screen 6).

2 · Notes — one shelf, a note selected

Master-detail over notes and documents together, told apart by a quiet glyph on the document rows. List groups: On your screen (stuck notes, recency-ordered), then day groups. A note opens the rich-text editor; header actions are dictate · copy · delete. The stick toggle is the floating capsule over the editor and its label is the state ("Stick to screen" ↔ "Unstick") — nothing else on screen says whether a note is stuck. Embedded dictation blocks carry a left rule + mic attribution. Add Note is the column's one capsule; Upload Audio… lives on the menu-bar right-click and the Dictations empty state.

3 · Notes — the same shelf, a document selected

Same list, different detail. A document (file, video or call capture) opens the tabbed Transcript | Summary reader; summaries exist only here. Actions: copy · delete — no stick (a document isn't something you put on your screen), no reveal-in-Finder (sources are often ephemeral) and no download (a .txt sidecar is already written automatically at transcription time).

4 · The sticky panel

Trace's chrome discipline: the tab strip is the only chrome — no titlebar band, text to the edges, formatting summoned over a selection rather than resident. Closing a tab unsticks that note; the panel disappears with the last tab, so there is no window close button. Tab order is creation order, never recency (recency changes on every keystroke and would reshuffle tabs mid-sentence). Two sizes, not free resize — the toggle at the strip's trailing edge swaps compact ⇄ expanded and anchors the top-left corner.

6 · Search everything

Only the Dictations pane's search crosses categories; the Notes pill filters its own shelf inline. Results replace the timeline, grouped Notes → Dictations → Documents, dated within each, matches highlighted. Note and document results both open on the Notes shelf; a dictation result returns to its day. Esc or ✕ returns to the day.

7 · Empty states

Every pane teaches its own verb with a concrete call to action. The Notes shelf holds two kinds of thing, so its empty state has to speak for both. Dictations also has a quiet-day variant for a past day that happens to be empty — distinct from a library that has nothing in it at all.

8 · Date navigation — month popover

The day label is a button. Chevrons step a day; the label opens a month with dots on days that have anything. Days after today are inert and dimmed — same rule as the forward chevron, since nothing can land there. Dots come from the same filter as the stream, so a dot can never open an empty day. "Jump to today" closes the loop.
